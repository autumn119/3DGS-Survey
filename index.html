UAV-Based Neural Reconstruction of Dong Drum Towers:
A Multi-Case Benchmark for Conservation-Oriented Method Selection

Jiaxing Tao

Department of Computer Science and Technology, Kaili College, Guizhou, China

🗃 Code & Benchmark 📎 Dataset (Zenodo) 🔗 BibTeX

Overview

Figure 1: We benchmark four neural reconstruction methods (NeRF, 2DGS, 3DGS, Instant-NGP) against a conventional SfM-MVS baseline on UAV imagery of four Dong minority drum towers (朝利 Chaoli, 从江 Congjiang, 则里 Zeli, 增冲 Zengchong), evaluating novel-view rendering, geometric consistency, and dark-region wooden-structure preservation for conservation-oriented method selection.

Abstract

Dong drum towers are timber landmarks of the Dong minority in Guizhou, China, whose intricate wooden frameworks — multi-tier eaves, imitation-dougong brackets, and chuandou frames — pose severe challenges to 3D digitization: complex geometry, occluded dark regions under the eaves, and variable outdoor lighting. This study establishes a multi-case benchmark that evaluates four neural reconstruction methods (NeRF, 2DGS, 3DGS, and Instant-NGP) together with a conventional SfM-MVS photogrammetric baseline on UAV and ground-level imagery of four drum towers. Under a unified, fairness-controlled protocol (identical image sets, shared COLMAP poses, common LLFF-style train/test splits, and a single RTX 4090 workstation), we evaluate (1) novel-view rendering quality (PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS), (2) training efficiency and rendering speed, (3) geometric consistency with the SfM-MVS reference (Accuracy / Recall / F1 / NCD / P95 on identically cropped tower-body point clouds), and (4) dark-region preservation of wooden structures via ground-level upward-facing imagery. 3DGS achieves the best rendering fidelity (mean PSNR 31.81 dB, SSIM 0.9646, LPIPS 0.0227), while INGP offers the fastest training; conventional MVS leaves systematic holes beneath the eaves that 3DGS reconstructs completely. The results provide actionable, conservation-oriented method-selection guidelines for heritage practitioners, and all code and data are released with a permanent Zenodo DOI.

Key Contributions

  • 📈
    Multi-Case Heritage Benchmark A fairness-controlled benchmark of four neural reconstruction methods plus an SfM-MVS baseline across four real Dong drum tower scenes, covering UAV aerial and ground-level upward-facing capture.
  • 📏
    Beyond PSNR: Geometric & Dark-Region Evaluation Geometric consistency with the photogrammetric reference (Accuracy / Recall / F1 / NCD / P95 on uniformly cropped tower bodies), plus a dark-region coverage analysis revealing where conventional MVS fails beneath the eaves.
  • 🏛
    Dual-Viewpoint Heritage Dataset UAV aerial corpus plus ground-level upward-facing imagery of wooden structures (Congjiang: 423 train / 61 test; Zengchong: 782 train / 112 test), with COLMAP reconstructions and train/test splits.
  • 💻
    Open-Source Release All benchmark code, per-method training wrappers, evaluation scripts, and the dataset are publicly available with a permanent Zenodo DOI (10.5281/zenodo.21429615) for long-term reproducibility.

Benchmark Protocol

Six pipeline stages with strict fairness control: identical image sets per scene, shared SfM camera poses, common train/test splits (every 8th frame held out), and default/recommended parameters per method on the same RTX 4090 workstation.

Stage 1–2 UAV Acquisition & Corpus Construction

Drone flights around four drum towers (544–654 images per scene) plus ground-level upward-facing capture for dark wooden structures.

Stage 3 Camera Poses (COLMAP)

SfM pose estimation and sparse reconstruction with COLMAP 3.14 (CUDA); 2DGS and 3DGS share the same COLMAP workspace.

Stage 4 Neural Reconstruction

Four methods trained per scene: NeRF (blender, factor 8), 2DGS (30k, sh=3), 3DGS (30k), and Instant-NGP (50k steps, hash encoding).

Stage 5 Multi-Dimensional Evaluation

Held-out rendering (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS), training time, FPS, geometric consistency (Acc/Recall/F1/NCD/P95 vs. MVS), dark-region coverage analysis.

Stage 6 Conservation-Oriented Interpretation

Method-selection guidelines translating quantitative findings into recommendations for heritage digitization practitioners.
